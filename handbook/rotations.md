# {% include icon.html icon="fa-solid fa-arrows-rotate" %}Rotation projects

Scripps graduate students usually spend 8–12 weeks rotating in a lab before choosing where to do
their thesis. This page explains how rotations work in the Su and Wu labs.

{% include section.html %}

## Finding a project

* **Start with a conversation.** Email Andrew or Chunlei. Have a look at our
  [research](/projects) first to see what interests you.
* **Projects build on work already underway.** That way you have people to learn from and code
  to start from.
* **Projects are sized to fit the rotation.** We aim for a question that can produce a result in
  8–12 weeks, such as a working prototype, an analysis or a benchmark, even if the result is
  preliminary.

{% include section.html %}

## When you start

* **You get mentors and a home channel.** You'll have one or two day-to-day mentors, plus a Slack
  channel for the project where you ask questions and post progress.
* **You get access.** We'll add you to the lab Slack, the
  [SuLab GitHub organization](https://github.com/orgs/SuLab/), the lab calendar and our
  [group meetings](/handbook/groupmeetings). If you need compute, ask Andrew or Chunlei about the
  lab servers.
* **The lab pays for your project's costs.** Most projects can use your institutional Claude
  subscription. If yours needs more, we'll set up an API key for it.
* **Your code goes on GitHub from day one.** Use a fork or branch of an existing lab repository,
  or a new repository in your own account. See our [coding standards](/handbook/coding-standards).
* **We schedule your end-of-rotation talk in your first week**, for a science meeting near the
  end of your rotation.
* **Lab policies apply to rotation students too**, including the
  [holiday and vacation policy](/handbook/holiday-and-vacation).

{% include section.html %}

## At the end

* **Present a recap of your project** at your science meeting, then add a link to your slides in
  the schedule. See [how to give a great talk](/handbook/giving-a-talk).
* **Write a 2-page report** that summarizes the work: what you set out to do, what you did, what
  worked, what didn't, and what you'd do next.
* **Leave your code so someone else can pick it up.** Push everything, and write a README that
  says how to run it.
* **If you join the lab**, you keep your access and we finish your
  [onboarding](/handbook/onboarding).
* **If you don't**, including if you go on to another rotation, we remove your lab access: GitHub,
  the calendar and the meeting invites. If you choose the lab later, we'll onboard you then.
